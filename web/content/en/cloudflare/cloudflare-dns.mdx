Cloudflare's original product is [DNS](/en/web-fundamentals/dns) hosting and a reverse-proxy/CDN layer sitting in front of a domain's actual traffic — nearly everything else in this section (Workers, Custom Domains, caching) depends on a domain already being an active Cloudflare zone.

A **proxied** record makes Cloudflare a genuine [reverse proxy](/en/backend/reverse-proxies) in front of the domain's real infrastructure — visitors resolve the domain to one of Cloudflare's own IPs, never the actual origin server's, which is also why a proxied domain's true origin IP being leaked elsewhere (an old DNS record, an exposed server header) can be a real security concern: it's the address every other protection is meant to keep hidden.

## Common record types

- **A / AAAA** — a hostname to an IPv4 or IPv6 address directly.
- **CNAME** — a hostname to *another* hostname, which is then resolved in turn — commonly used to point a subdomain at a platform-managed hostname without needing to know or hardcode its IP.
- **MX** — where email for a domain should be delivered, entirely separate from where its website is served.
- **TXT** — arbitrary text, commonly used for domain ownership verification and email authentication (SPF, DKIM) records.

## Why a Worker needs an active zone

A [Workers Route](/en/cloudflare/workers-routes) or [Custom Domain](/en/cloudflare/custom-domains) only works on a domain Cloudflare is already authoritative for — Cloudflare has to be the thing actually resolving and routing that domain's traffic before it can hand any of it off to a Worker. This is why deploying a Worker to a new domain always starts with pointing that domain's nameservers at Cloudflare first.
