ACID is the set of four guarantees a properly implemented [transaction](/en/databases/transactions) is supposed to provide. It's a checklist for what "safe" actually means for a database write — each letter covers a specific way a naive implementation could otherwise corrupt or lose data.

## Isolation levels: a deliberately tunable guarantee

Full isolation (every transaction behaving as if it ran completely alone) is expensive to implement — engines offer weaker, faster isolation levels as an explicit trade-off, and picking one is a real engineering decision, not just an academic distinction.

## Not every database chooses full ACID

Some databases (particularly many [NoSQL](/en/databases/nosql) stores) deliberately relax one or more of these guarantees — usually consistency or isolation — in exchange for availability or throughput across many machines. This isn't a defect; it's a legitimate trade-off for workloads where eventual consistency is acceptable and the coordination cost of full ACID across a distributed system isn't worth paying.
