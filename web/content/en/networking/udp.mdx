**UDP (User Datagram Protocol)** sends a datagram and moves on — no handshake, no acknowledgment, no retransmission, no guaranteed order. That sounds strictly worse than [TCP](/en/networking/tcp) until you notice what it buys back: none of TCP's setup latency or per-segment bookkeeping, which matters enormously for traffic where a late packet is worse than a missing one.

## A much smaller header

```text
Source Port | Destination Port
Length
Checksum
```

That's the entire header — eight bytes, versus TCP's twenty-plus. There's no sequence number because UDP never reorders anything, and no window size because UDP never throttles itself; an application that needs either has to implement it itself on top.

## Reliability becomes the application's job

Nothing stops an application from building acknowledgments, retransmission, or ordering on top of UDP — QUIC (which HTTP/3 runs on) does exactly this, reimplementing the reliability pieces it wants while keeping UDP's low setup cost and avoiding TCP's head-of-line blocking, where one lost segment stalls everything behind it.
