**Authentication** answers exactly one question: *is this really who it claims to be?* It's easy to conflate with [authorization](/en/security/authorization) ("what is this identity allowed to do") because they usually happen back to back, but they're independent — a system can authenticate someone perfectly and still refuse them access to something, and vice versa a broken authorization check can leak data to a perfectly legitimately authenticated user.

## Multi-factor authentication

**MFA** requires evidence from at least two *different* factor categories — a password plus an authenticator app code, not a password plus a security question, since both of those are "something you know" and a phisher who gets one likely gets the other too. This is why MFA meaningfully raises the bar: compromising two independent categories of evidence is far harder than compromising one, even a strong one.

## What "logged in" actually means afterward

Authentication itself is a single moment — the login request succeeding. Staying recognized across every subsequent request is a separate mechanism, typically [sessions](/en/security/sessions) or a [JWT](/en/security/jwt); authentication proves identity once, and one of those carries proof of that identity forward.
